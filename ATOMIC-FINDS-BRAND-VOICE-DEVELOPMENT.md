## Overview

This document provides a structured workflow for developing Atomic Finds' **mission, core values, and content style guidelines** using Perplexity's research capabilities. The output will drive all written content across the platform: website copy, blog posts, social media, and product descriptions.

**Note:** This project builds on three foundational documents already in development:
1. **Product Taxonomy** — Product types, tags, keywords, curator assignments
2. **Rattan Deep Dive** — Cultural, botanical, and ethical context for woven materials
3. **Website Copy Approach** — Sitewide tone rules, copy hierarchy, page templates

The task of developing mission/values is to **synthesize these existing insights into a cohesive brand philosophy** that guides voice, tone, and content strategy across all channels.

**Prompt to Perplexity:**

> *"Based on the three Atomic Finds ATX foundation documents I've just shared (product taxonomy, rattan deep dive, and website copy approach), synthesize a mission statement and core values framework for the brand.*

> *Key context to embed:*
> *- Atomic Finds ATX is owned and run by Jennyfer Gomez, a Mexican-American curator and restorer*
> *- The brand philosophy centers on authentication, restoration, and cultural accountability (see rattan deep dive for the cultural context)*
> *- The tagline is 'Far-out finds, down-to-earth prices' (80% grounded furniture expertise, 15% warmth, 5% sparkle)*
> *- Four playful Curators (Daisy, Milo, Tatiana, Malibu) guide customers by function/feeling, but Jennyfer is the real authority*
> *- The voice prioritizes authenticity, specificity, and honesty about vintage condition over hype*
> *- Sustainability is positioned as 'radical sustainability through curation,' not greenwashing*
> *- Austin is integral to identity (local pickup, sourcing, community, creative heritage)*
> *
> *Deliver:*
> *1. A single mission statement (1-2 sentences) that captures the soul of the business and honors Jennyfer's role*
> *2. 4-5 core values (names + 1-sentence descriptions) that differentiate Atomic Finds from typical vintage furniture sellers*
> *3. For each value, provide 3 concrete examples of how it shows up in daily operations (customer communication, product sourcing, curation decisions, website copy)*
> *4. For each value, note what anti-values or misconceptions the brand actively rejects (e.g., 'Authenticity vs. Greenwashing')*
> *5. Map how each value connects back to specific sections of the Rattan Deep Dive (cultural accountability, colonial extraction awareness, BIPOC reclamation, etc.)*"

### Expected Output Structure

**Mission Statement** (should reflect):
- Jennyfer as the real curator/restorer (not the Curators)
- Authentic 1970s-80s vintage focus
- Austin grounding
- Respect for material origins and cultural roots
- Restoration as core practice (not just selling)

*Example skeleton:* "To [action] authentic vintage rattan, bamboo, and mid-century pieces by [Jennyfer's method], honoring [cultural/craft respect], and serving [customer base]."

**Core Values** (should include):
- **Authenticity & Specificity** — Every piece documented honestly (material, era, condition, restoration). Anti-value: vague greenwashing, "pre-owned" euphemisms.
- **Curation Over Commerce** — Handpicked by Jennyfer, not mass-sourced. Quality-first. Anti-value: volume-driven dropshipping, generic vintage.
- **Cultural Accountability** — Acknowledging origins, honoring global artisans, combating colonial erasure (tie to rattan deep dive). Anti-value: "exotic" marketing tropes, sanitized cultural appropriation.
- **Radical Sustainability** — Extending life of well-made objects, keeping pieces out of landfills, supporting BIPOC ownership of curation (Austin-specific). Anti-value: fast-furniture consumption, corporate-branded "eco-friendly" new items.
- **Accessibility & Joy** — Beautiful, lasting furniture at prices people can afford. Without preciousness or gatekeeping. Anti-value: luxury gatekeeping, "museum piece" untouchability.

## Part 2: Blog & Website Copy Style Guide

### Research Brief for Perplexity

**Prompt to Perplexity:**

> *"Develop a comprehensive blog, website, and long-form content style guide for Atomic Finds ATX based on this existing copy approach document I'm sharing.*
>
> *The existing 'Full Website Copy Approach' establishes a voice rule: '80% grounded furniture expertise, 15% warmth and personality, 5% space-age sparkle.' It also specifies:*
> *- Required words: handpicked, restored, 1970s, mid-century, rattan, bamboo, wicker, patina, built to last, ready for its forever home, Austin*

> *2. Blog Content Pillars & Angles*
> *- Design History (era/material context, movements, artistry)*
> *- Sourcing Stories (Jennyfer's hunts, how pieces are found, decision-making)*
> *- Cultural & Material Context (tie to rattan deep dive: colonial history, artisan roots, Austin community)*

> *- Opening hooks for each content pillar (5+ examples)*
> *- Middle structure: balance of narrative, specificity, and takeaway*
> *- How to weave in SEO keywords naturally (use this keyword bank: [from product taxonomy])*
> *- How to close with action (link to piece, invite questions, encourage discovery)*
> *
> *4. Website Copy Template Expansion*
> *- Homepage hero variations for seasonal/thematic rotations*
> *- About page depth: telling Jennyfer's story without overshadowing the work*

> *- Journal/blog index page that sets expectations for content quality*

> *- 5 complete blog post examples (600-1000 words each), showing all 5 content pillars*
> *- Homepage hero variations (3-4 alternatives to rotate seasonally)*
> *- About page long-form (800-1000 words, balancing Jennyfer's personal story with mission)*
> *- A one-page quick-reference for all writers/contributors covering tone, word choices, structure*
> *- A content calendar template showing how to distribute topics across 12 weeks*"

### Expected Output Structure

**Tone & Voice Formula (Expanded):**
- **The 80/15/5 principle:** 80% grounded expertise (material, era, construction, condition), 15% warmth (approachable, conversational), 5% sparkle (cosmic, whimsical—"far-out," "orbit," "signal," "universe")
- **Voice baseline:** Knowledgeable friend, not algorithm. Opinionated about craftsmanship. Use "we" for brand voice, "I" when Jennyfer speaks directly.
- **Required vocabulary:** handpicked, restored, 1970s, mid-century, rattan, bamboo, wicker, patina, built to last, ready for its forever home, Austin
- **Forbidden:** pre-owned, refurbished, luxury marketing language, overblown claims ("one-of-a-kind amazing"), fake scarcity, long cosmic metaphors (keep space metaphor to 5%)
- **Sentence structure:** Short, punchy, declarative. Contractions welcome. Specificity over adjectives.

**Blog Post Content Pillars & Headlines:**
- **Design History** — "The Physics of the Peacock Chair: Why This Shape Became Iconic" / "1970s Rattan Wasn't Trend; It Was Revolution"
- **Sourcing Stories** — "Three Hours in an Estate Sale: How I Found the Dresser Everyone Walks Past" / "Why I Passed on 200 Pieces to Bring Home 3"
- **Cultural Context** — "Rattan Isn't Exotic—It's Colonial. Here's What That Means." / "What Your Vintage Wicker Dresser Owes to Philippines Artisans"
- **Restoration & Care** — "Reading a Joint: How to Know If Your Vintage Chair is Actually Solid" / "The First Thing You Should Do With Every Wicker Find"
- **Styling & Inspiration** — "This Chair Doesn't Go Anywhere Until Your Space Asks for It" / "Pairing Rattan with Modern Minimalism (It Works)"
- **Sustainability** — "Radical Sustainability Looks Like This: One Dresser, Two Families" / "Why 'Eco-Friendly New' is a Lie Vintage Already Solved"
- **Behind-the-Scenes** — "A Saturday Morning Restoration: From Stripping Polyurethane to Exposed Grain" / "Here's Why I Rejected This Rattan Frame (And You Should Too)"

**Website Copy Principles:**
- **Homepage:** Open with atmosphere/vibe ("Vintage, written in the stars"), then move to specifics (what people get, how to start)
- **About:** Jennyfer's personal story (sourcing methodology, eye, aesthetic) + the mission (why curation matters, what the brand stands for)
- **Collections:** Lead with functional benefit ("Seating with a story"), explain who it's for, close with discovery CTA
- **Blog index:** Signal quality, show the breadth of content, invite subscription/follow

### Expected Output Structure

**Product Description Template:**

**[Hook — The Mood]**  
"This isn't just a chair. It's a corner that became somewhere. 1970s rattan with lines that whisper 'sit here and think.'"

**[Story — The History]**  
"Hand-woven rattan in the boho-coastal style that defined 70s interiors. The construction suggests studio/designer-maker era—careful joints, solid frame, meant to last decades. Honey-toned finish with that lived-in patina that only comes with time."

**[Technical — The Facts]**  
"Materials: Hand-woven rattan, wooden frame (appears to be oak), original brass-plated hardware. Dimensions: 32in W x 28in D x 38in H. Seat height: 18in. Condition: Structurally solid, no wobble, joints tight. Finish shows age-appropriate patina and minor wear (see photos). One spot of minor water staining on back leg (cosmetic, documented in photos)."

**[The Ask — Who It's For]**  
"This lives best next to a window, a lamp, and a stack of books. Or in a bedroom corner where morning light finds it. Or—and we love this—in a creative studio space where it's both functional seating and visual anchor. It works in bohemian homes, mid-century modern spaces, eclectic collectors' homes."

**[Honesty — What Makes It Real]**  
"This is not new. It carries its 50 years openly. That's not a flaw—it's proof. It's been cared for enough to still be here, beautiful, waiting for someone who gets it."

## Part 5: Implementation & Consistency

### How to Use These Guidelines

1. **Create a Living Document**
   - House all guidelines in one place (Notion, Google Doc, or in the CMS itself)
   - Make it accessible to Jennyfer, Anthony, and any future team members
   - Update quarterly as the brand voice evolves

2. **Apply to All Touchpoints**
   - Product descriptions in Showroom
   - Blog posts on the Press Office
   - Social media captions (schedule via Postiz or native platforms)
   - Website pages (home, about, collections)
   - Email communications
   - Customer support responses

3. **Review & Refine**
   - After 3 months of using guidelines, audit: Do captions feel authentic? Do customers respond? Does the voice feel consistent?
   - Every 6 months, review trending topics in vintage/design space and update content pillars
   - Track which blog post angles and social posts perform best—lean into what resonates

4. **Template Assets**
   - Create Notion/Google Docs templates for each content type
   - Build Canva templates for social graphics that reinforce visual voice
   - Set up email templates for customer communications
   - Create a swipe file of great examples for reference

## Part 6: Measurement & Iteration

### KPIs to Track

**Blog/Website:**
- Page views, time on page, bounce rate
- Shares (do people forward this to friends?)
- Comments (does it spark conversation?)
- Ranking for target keywords (1970s rattan, vintage MCM, etc.)

**Social Media:**
- Engagement rate (comments > likes; real conversation > vanity metrics)
- Click-through rate to website/shop
- Follower growth (slower but steadier = better quality audience)
- Saves/shares (signals people want to reference it later)

**Products:**
- Conversion rate by description style
- Customer questions in DMs (if lots of "Is this real wood?" you're not describing materials clearly enough)
- Returns/satisfaction (are customers getting what they expected from descriptions?)

## Appendix: Voice Reference Checklist

**Before publishing ANY content, ask:**

- [ ] Does this sound like a conversation with someone who knows and cares about design?
- [ ] Did I show up as the brand, or did I hide behind corporate speak?
- [ ] Would Jennyfer say this? (If no, rethink it)
- [ ] Did I earn my opinion? (Facts first, opinions built on them)
- [ ] Is there a story here, or just info?
- [ ] What am I asking the reader to *feel*, not just *buy*?
- [ ] Is this honest? (Including about flaws, limitations, what this piece isn't)
- [ ] Could only Atomic Finds say this? (If any competitor could say it, dig deeper)
