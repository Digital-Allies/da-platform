## Welcome

This is a short guide to the AI workspace Anthony built for Atomic Finds. Think of it as an assistant that helps with the time-consuming parts of running the shop — research, drafting, organizing — so you have more hours back for finding great pieces and growing the business.

You don't need to know anything technical to use it. You just talk to it like you're describing a piece to a friend, and it does the legwork.

**The one thing to remember above everything else:** this assistant drafts and suggests — it never decides, prices, or publishes anything on its own. Everything comes back to you for a final look before it goes anywhere public.

## 2. The Ground Rules (so you always know what to trust)

Straight from the AI Permissions questionnaire Anthony's finalizing with you — this is the short version:

**AI can:**
- Organize and transcribe (turn your notes, labels, and photos into a clean record)
- Summarize and draft (descriptions, captions, research notes)
- Flag when something's missing before it becomes a problem

**AI can never, on its own:**
- Make up facts about a piece — era, maker, materials, condition
- Set or change a price
- Decide something is safe, authentic, or "fully restored"
- Publish anything or promise a buyer anything
- Use a customer's private info without your OK

If the AI isn't sure about something, it should tell you and ask — not guess. If a draft ever states something as fact that you didn't confirm, that's a bug, not a feature — tell Anthony.

**Bottom line:** you're always the final word on price, condition claims, and what goes live. The AI's job is to get you a strong first draft, fast, so your job is reviewing and deciding — not starting from a blank page.

## 3. What You'll Actually Use It For

### Workflow 1 — Research & Sourcing
When you've got a piece (or you're deciding whether to pick one up), describe what you see — or share photos — and ask the assistant to help you:
- Identify likely era, materials, and maker cues to look into
- Pull together comparable listings so you have a sense of the market
- Put together a short checklist of what to verify before you commit (condition, structural issues, missing hardware, etc.)

Everything it finds gets treated as a lead to verify, not a confirmed fact — you're still the one who signs off on what a piece actually is.

### Workflow 2 — Naming Your Finds
Once a piece is home and photographed, the assistant helps you turn rough notes into a few title and description options, using the same formula the site already runs on:

> **[Era] [Material] [Style] | [Unique Feature] | [Condition/Restoration] | [Who It's For]**
>
> Example: *"1970s rattan coffee table with natural woven base and glass top. Brings warmth and texture without feeling precious. Cleaned and reinforced. Perfect for any living room that needs character."*

You describe the piece in your own words (even messy, half-finished sentences are fine) — it hands back a few polished options for you to pick from, tweak, or reject outright.

### Workflow 3 — Publish Everywhere at Once *(coming soon)*
The next piece Anthony's building: one workflow that takes an approved listing and preps it for every platform you sell on — the website, Facebook Marketplace, Instagram — instead of retyping the same piece three times. Not live yet, but it's the direction this is all heading, and it's built on the same rule as everything else: nothing goes out until you approve it.

## 4. Try It: Your First Piece

The best way to get comfortable is to run one real item through the whole loop:

1. Pick a piece you already have that isn't listed yet.
2. Open your AI workspace and describe it — condition, materials, anything you know about where it came from.
3. Ask for a few title/description options using the formula above.
4. Read them like a customer would. Fix anything that doesn't sound like you, or doesn't match what you actually know about the piece.
5. Once you're happy with it, that's what goes into the Showroom (see the CMS guide for the "Add a Product" steps).

That's the whole loop — describe, review, approve. Everything else is a variation on this.

## 5. Something Feels Off?

If a draft states something you never told it, if it feels pushy about a price, or if anything about this workflow is confusing — that's exactly what Anthony wants to hear about, not something to just work around.

Email **contact@digitalallies.net** with what happened and, if you can, what the AI said. We'll fix the workflow, not just that one draft.
