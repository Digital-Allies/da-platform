## 1. Copy System

### The job of the copy

Every page should do at least one of four things:

1. Help a shopper picture the piece in their home.
2. Prove why the piece is special: material, era, construction, restoration, and condition.
3. Make vintage feel approachable rather than precious or intimidating.
4. Give a clear next step: explore, inquire, claim, schedule a viewing, or arrange pickup.

### Voice rules

**Use:** handpicked, restored, 1970s, mid-century, rattan, bamboo, wicker, patina, built to last, ready for its forever home, Austin.

**Avoid:** pre-owned, refurbished, generic luxury language, overblown claims, fake scarcity, and long cosmic metaphors.

**Tone formula:** 80% grounded furniture expertise, 15% warmth and personality, 5% space-age sparkle.

### Copy hierarchy

- **Eyebrow:** short utility label, typically all caps: `NEWLY LANDED` or `THE COLLECTION`
- **Headline:** direct and visual; lead with the benefit or category
- **Script accent:** one short, emotional line only
- **Body:** two or three plainspoken sentences; prioritize specifics
- **CTA:** active, conversational, and decisive

### Primary CTA library

- `[ Explore Collection ]`
- `[ View Full Catalog ]`
- `[ See New Arrivals ]`
- `[ Ask About This Piece ]`
- `[ Claim This Find ]`
- `[ Schedule a Viewing ]`
- `[ Get in Touch ]`
- `[ Learn Jennyfer’s Story ]`

Use `Claim This Find` only for truly purchasable, one-of-one inventory. Use `Ask About This Piece` or `Schedule a Viewing` for higher-priced, pickup-only, or inquiry-led pieces.

- 1970s Rattan Coffee Table with Glass Top
- Vintage Wicker Barrel Chair

This [era] [material] [product type] brings [visual or functional benefit] to any [room/use]. Its [specific feature] gives it the kind of character that is hard to fake—and even harder to find twice.

This 1970s rattan coffee table brings warmth and texture to a living room without making the whole space feel too precious. Its woven base and glass top keep the silhouette light, while the natural patina gives it the kind of character that cannot be mass-produced.

## 12. Final Editorial Guardrails

- Name Jennyfer when discussing sourcing, restoration, ownership, or expertise.
- Use The Curators to add personality and product discovery—not to replace the human story.
- Keep every product description specific: material, era, silhouette, condition, restoration, room fit, and measurements.
- Let the visuals carry most of the cosmic mood; copy should remain clear and useful.
- Use one script-like emotional line per section at most.
- Treat all availability as temporary and all claims as subject to confirmation until payment or hold status is confirmed.
